## PHASE 2: SCORE-BOOSTING STRATEGIES (Days 1–30)

### Credit Utilization (30% of FICO Score)
- **Goal:** Get utilization below 10% on each card (ideal) or below 30% (minimum)
- Pay down revolving balances aggressively
- If you have cards near their limit, paying them down even partially = fast score jump
- **Trick:** Ask your card issuers for a credit limit increase (soft pull only — ask specifically for a soft pull)

### Payment History (35% of FICO Score — Biggest Factor)
- Set up autopay on every account so you never miss a payment going forward
- Even one on-time payment per month starts rebuilding this factor
- If you have accounts past-due, bring them current immediately

### Chime Credit Builder / Secured Cards
- Your Chime screenshot shows pending bills — **activate Boost** for all utilities you can
- Consider a secured card (Chime, Discover Secured, Capital One Secured) — deposit $200-500 and use it for small purchases, pay in full monthly

### Authorized User Strategy
- Ask a family member or friend with excellent credit (750+, low utilization, old account) to add you as an **authorized user** on their oldest credit card
- Their positive history can appear on your report and boost your score
- You don't need to use or even receive the card

### Credit Mix (10% of FICO)
- Having both revolving (credit cards) and installment (loans) accounts helps
- Consider a **Credit Builder Loan** from Self.inc, DCU, or a local credit union (~$25/month, builds payment history)

## PHASE 3: BUREAU-SPECIFIC DISPUTE STRATEGIES (Days 1–45)

### Key Legal Leverage Points

**1. The "5-Day Rule" (FCRA § 611(a)(1))**
If a bureau fails to respond within 30 days (or 45 with documentation), the disputed item **must be deleted**. Track your certified mail delivery dates religiously.

**2. Re-Aging Violations**
Collections agencies sometimes reset the clock by updating the DOFD to a newer date. This is illegal under FCRA § 623(a)(5). If you catch this, the item is immediately deletable and you may have a claim for damages.

**3. The "Debt Validation" Play (FDCPA § 809)**
For any collection account, **also send a Debt Validation Letter** directly to the collection agency within 30 days of their first contact. They must stop all collection activity and reporting until they validate. If they can't validate, they must delete.

**4. Obsolete Information (FCRA § 605)**
- Negative items: 7 years from DOFD
- Chapter 7 Bankruptcy: 10 years
- Hard inquiries: 2 years
- Paid tax liens: 7 years
- Anything past these dates must be removed — period.

**5. Furnisher Direct Dispute (FCRA § 623(a)(8))**
You can dispute directly with the furnisher (original creditor or collector), not just the bureau. The furnisher then has its own obligation to investigate and correct.

**6. Goodwill Letters**
For accounts you've paid in full with a single late payment, write a **goodwill letter** to the original creditor (not the bureau) asking them to remove the late payment as a courtesy. Many will, especially longtime customers.

**7. Pay-for-Delete**
For collection accounts, negotiate with the collector: offer to pay (or settle for less) **in exchange for a written agreement to delete the tradeline** from all three bureaus. **Get this in writing before paying.**

## PHASE 4: CFPB & ESCALATION (If Bureaus Don't Comply)

### If Bureau Doesn't Respond in 30 Days:
1. File CFPB complaint at **consumerfinance.gov/complaint**
2. File FTC complaint at **reportfraud.ftc.gov**
3. File complaint with your **State Attorney General**
4. Consult a **consumer protection attorney** — many take FCRA cases on contingency (you pay nothing unless they win)

### FCRA Lawsuit Threat / Reality
- Willful FCRA violations: $100–$1,000 statutory damages per violation + punitive + attorney fees
- Negligent violations: actual damages + attorney fees
- Many attorneys take these on contingency — the bureaus know this and often settle
- The **National Association of Consumer Advocates (NACA)** at naca.net can refer you to a qualified FCRA attorney

## IMPORTANT LEGAL NOTES

1. **Credit repair companies** must comply with the **Credit Repair Organizations Act (CROA), 15 U.S.C. § 1679 et seq.** — they cannot charge upfront fees, must give you a 3-day right to cancel, and cannot make guarantees they can't keep.

2. **You have the same rights as any credit repair company** — you can dispute everything yourself for free. The three bureaus must give you the same investigation as any paid service.

3. **Disputing accurate information is not effective and could be considered fraud** — only dispute items that are genuinely inaccurate, unverifiable, incomplete, or obsolete.

4. **Never pay a collector without a written pay-for-delete agreement** — paying a collection without this agreement will not remove the tradeline; it just changes it from "unpaid" to "paid collection," which still hurts your score.
